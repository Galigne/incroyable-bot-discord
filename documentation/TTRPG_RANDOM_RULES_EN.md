# Overview

JDR Random, a **tabletop roleplaying game** ([TTRPG](#ttrpg)), aims for an **accessible**, **creative**, and clearly **fun-first** experience. The rules are simple for players, and preparation for the **Game Master** ([GM](#gm)) stays quick: the goal is to encourage **improvisation**, **freedom**, and **enjoyment** rather than mechanical complexity.

At the start of a [campaign](#campaign), the world map is intentionally sparse: the [GM](#gm) places the main points of interest and leaves the rest blank. Players then complete the world by creating their characters; the [GM](#gm), or random generators, enrich the rest as play goes on. In later campaigns, the map, the characters, and the previous [lore](#lore) can be kept to deepen the setting.

No race and no class are imposed: players invent their race, appearance, traits, history, and [racial traits](#racial-traits), as well as their class, stats, weapons, armor, magic, talents, and [background](#background). Any element added to the setting must be **validated by the [GM](#gm)** to guarantee **consistency** and **balance**.

There is no absolute restriction on your actions, nor are there lists of spells or items doomed to repeat from game to game. Here, **your imagination sets the limits**. JDR Random leaves real-time balancing to the [GM](#gm), giving everyone a great deal of creative freedom.

For [GM](#gm) preparation before each session, random generators make it easier to improvise quests, events, loot, characters, and more. That way, neither the players nor the [GM](#gm) are pressured into following an overly rigid story.

The spirit of the game is **relaxed**: embrace the absurd and the unexpected (`chicken-demons`, schizophrenic vampires, etc.). The goal is to **have fun**, not to optimize every decision mechanically.

# Character Creation

## Character Identity and Intentions

### Backstory and Goals

You must detail **your character's backstory**: where they come from, how they grew up, what they were doing before the adventure began, what happened to them recently, and what they know about the world.

To help you, you can answer a few simple questions:

- Where were they born? In what kind of environment (`noble`, `peasant`, `street`, `temple`, `guild`, `tribe`, etc.)?
- What major events marked their life (`war`, `betrayal`, `miracle`, `accident`, `important meeting`, etc.)?
- What are their ties (`family`, `friends`, `mentor`, `enemies`, `debts`, `promises`)?
- What can they actually do (`trades`, `studies`, `travel`, `military experience`, `magic`, `crimes`, etc.)?

<u>Goals</u>

- **Short term**: what do they want to gain in the next adventures (`money`, `information`, `revenge`, `recognition`, `protecting someone`, etc.)?
- **Long term**: what is their deeper goal (`glory`, `fortune`, `righting an injustice`, `becoming the greatest mage in the kingdom`, `finding a loved one`, `protecting their homeland`, etc.)?

You can also give them **a secret**, shared with the [GM](#gm): a past wrongdoing, a hidden identity, a curse, a pact, etc. This gives the [GM](#gm) material to build plots around you.

Without taking things too seriously, try to remain **consistent with the setting**: a peasant from a small village does not wield ancient magic like an archmage, and a wanted thief does not walk around openly without consequences.

### Personality

Give your character substance by defining **their personality**: their character traits, mannerisms, habits, beliefs, qualities, and flaws. This will help you bring them to life during play.

You can note, for example:

- **Traits**: brave, shy, arrogant, cheerful, suspicious, sarcastic, naive, etc.
- **Habits / quirks**: always chews a blade of grass, collects strange coins, never trusts magic, etc.
- **Ideals**: justice, freedom, loyalty, power, knowledge, protection of the weak, etc.
- **Bonds**: attached to their family, guild, god, a cause, an oath, etc.
- **Flaws**: jealous, hot-tempered, greedy, paranoid, prideful, brawler, alcoholic, etc.

Also think about **their moral [alignment](#alignment)**: are they more good, selfish, cruel, disciplined, chaotic? You can use a simple axis (`Good / Neutral / Evil`) or a short description such as *idealistic but impulsive* or *selfish but loyal to their friends*.

### Group Consistency

Discuss your character with **the [GM](#gm) and the other players**. It is preferable for the group to have broadly compatible goals. A character who wants to kill everyone else or constantly sabotage the team risks ruining the game.

**[PvP](#pvp)** exists, but it is better to avoid having to remake a character before the adventure has even begun.

### Roleplay

Try to remain **consistent with your character** in your actions: a coward avoids frontal fights, a religious fanatic will not casually insult their god, etc.

<span style="color: darkorange;">Above all, do not make them deliberately rush into a deadly trap or into a situation with absolutely no chance of survival.</span>

Your character can be reckless, but like a real person, they generally value their life. You are not the main character: there will be no *deus ex machina* to save you. So do not be surprised if the [GM](#gm) asks you to create a new character.

## Race, Stats, and Magic

### Race

Your race can be entirely invented or taken from the universe created by your [GM](#gm). You can also draw inspiration from the great classics (`elves`, `dwarves`, `humans`, etc.) and reinterpret them in your own way.

Each race must include:

- a **name**;
- a **physical description**;
- **typical psychological traits**;
- a **history**, a **culture**, a **way of life**, and **relations with the world**;
- one or more **home cities** tied to the character;
- **[racial traits](#racial-traits)**.

### Racial Traits

Racial traits are characteristics shared by members of the same race. They may notably grant:

- a **skill bonus**: `+1` to a specific [roll](#roll) (`stealth`, `intimidation`, etc.);
- a **physical ability**: night vision, water breathing, greater endurance, etc.

### Base Stats

- Stats range from `4` (`minimum`) to `20` (`maximum`).
- A character starts at **level `1`** with **`67` points** to distribute.
- Ordinary [NPCs](#npc) (`villagers`, `merchants`, etc.) generally have stats between `6` and `8`.

### Point Cost by Value

- `0 to 14`: `1 point` per level
- `15 to 16`: `2 points` per level
- `17 to 18`: `3 points` per level
- `19 to 20`: `4 points` per level

### List of Stats

<a id="constitution"></a>
<u>Constitution</u>

Determines **[hit points](#hit-points-hp)**, resistance to conditions (`poison`, `paralysis`, `cold`, `heat`, etc.), and the character's carrying capacity.

<a id="strength"></a>
<u>Strength</u>

Affects the use of heavy [weapons](#weapons), blocking, and all actions tied to brute force: carrying, throwing heavy objects, breaking an object, etc.

<a id="dexterity"></a>
<u>Dexterity</u>

Affects the use of light and ranged [weapons](#weapons), dodging, and actions tied to dexterity: infiltration, pickpocketing, acrobatics, etc.

<a id="intelligence"></a>
<u>Intelligence</u>

Determines mastery of [RULE](#rule), [RULE Points](#rule-points), and actions tied to intelligence: general knowledge, understanding, thinking, etc.

<a id="speed"></a>
<u>Speed</u>

Determines [initiative](#initiative), [reflexes](#reflexes), movement distance, and actions tied to speed: catching someone, fleeing, drawing a weapon, etc.

<a id="perception"></a>
<u>Perception</u>

Improves aim, the five senses, instincts, and all actions tied to perception: spotting an enemy, detecting a lie, etc.

<a id="charisma"></a>
<u>Charisma</u>

Affects public ease, social interactions, bargaining, self-control, emotional management, intimidation, leadership, etc.

### Derived Stats

<a id="initiative"></a>
<u>Initiative</u>

Determines turn order in combat. `Initiative = Speed`

<a id="reflexes"></a>
<u>Reflexes</u>

Measure the ability to react quickly to unexpected danger. `Reflexes = Speed`

### RULE

A **RULE** is a magical mastery (`fire`, `telekinesis`, `illusion`, `animal control`, etc.).

- RULEs must be **described precisely** by the player; their limits and costs evolve with their level.
- There is no closed spell list. However, some scrolls can contain **instances of magic** usable by anyone.
- RULEs must reflect an aspect of your character's life or experiences. For example, someone who controls plants probably has a strong bond with nature, perhaps because they grew up in a forest.

### Example: Fire RULE (`level 1`)

- The character can generate flames reaching **`900 °C`** and move them at will, as long as they respect the imposed limits and maintain **line of sight**.
- The flames cannot appear beyond **`10 meters`** from the mage.
- Flame size decreases with distance:
  - in contact with the mage: a ball about **`20 cm`** in diameter;
  - at `10 m`: a **faint spark**.
- Creating the flames costs **`2 AP`**.
- The flames cannot burn their creator.
- Each [turn](#turn), the mage must spend **`1 AP`** to keep them active. This does not apply to real flames already generated by ignition.
- If the flames have been moved away, their size decreases at the start of the [turn](#turn), proportionally to the distance.
- The mage can control the flames and what they contain for **`2 AP`**, within a radius of **`10 meters`**.
- Control speed and precision decrease with distance:
  - in contact: about **`20 km/h`**;
  - at `10 m`: about **`5 km/h`**.
- The flames:
  - have no impact;
  - have no weight;
  - cannot push an opponent back;
  - cannot explode;
  - deal no blunt damage.

> **Usage example**: the mage can carry **charcoal balls** or **oil-soaked cloth** to add weight, create impact, or make targets easier to ignite.

Do not limit yourself to this example if you want a fire-based RULE. Discuss it with your [GM](#gm): your flames may have a unique color, be uncontrollable but applicable to your equipment, be stronger at close range, or have movement that depends on their intensity.

### RULE Points

RULE Points are used to create a new RULE or increase the level of an existing RULE (`1 point = level 1`, `2 points = level 2`, etc.). RULE Points are granted based on [intelligence](#intelligence): one RULE Point is gained at each of these thresholds: `10`, `12`, `14`, `16`, `18`, `20`.

### Talents

Your character starts with **`1 talent`**. Talents are varied and must remain **consistent with your character's story, personality, and lived experience**.

> **Examples**:
>
> - **Cold Immunity**: cannot be frozen.
> - **Assassin**: damage `x1.5` if the enemy has not spotted you.
> - **Bodybuilder**: `+1` to actions tied to [strength](#strength).
> - **Politician**: `+2` to [charisma](#charisma) during negotiations.

## Starting Equipment and Resources

### Starting Equipment

Your equipment includes **[weapons](#weapons)**, **[armor](#armor-and-protection)**, and everything intended for combat.

- You may choose **one basic armor**: light, medium, or heavy. Its appearance is up to you. Capes, shoes, amulets, and other jewelry **grant no effect** unless they are magical.
- You start with **up to `3` basic main weapons** of your choice (`sword`, `shield`, `spear`, `bow`, `scepter`, etc.).
- You may also take a few **secondary weapons** or **miscellaneous tools** (`throwing knives`, `hatchet`, `bear trap`, `poison`, `chains`, etc.), in limited quantity.
- **Arrows**, **bolts**, and other ammunition for an unmodified main weapon are **unlimited**.
- You may start with **`1` weak magical item**, provided its impact is **negligible** and **accepted by the [GM](#gm)**.

### Starting Inventory

- All players start with **`2 healing potions`**, each restoring **[`50 HP`](#hit-points-hp)**.
- Determine starting gold: roll `1D20 + 5`.
- You may carry various items (`lantern`, `instrument`, `lockpicking kit`, etc.), provided they are **consistent with the character** and in reasonable quantity.

# Action Resolution

## Rolls

The system mainly relies on rolls **equal to or below** the targeted stat.

- **Key rule**: to succeed, you must get a result **lower than or equal to** the target.
- **`1`** is the best possible result (**[critical success](#critical-success)**).
- **`20`** is the worst possible result (**[critical failure](#critical-failure)**).

### Types of Dice

You will mainly use:

- **D2** (`coin`) for simple two-option decisions (`yes/no`, `heads/tails`, `success/failure`, etc.).
- **D20** for the majority of **stat rolls** ([strength](#strength), [dexterity](#dexterity), etc.).
- **D100** for **percentage chances** (`10 %` chance to poison the target, `30 %` chance to stun an enemy, etc.).

### Standard Roll

For a standard [stat roll](#roll):

1. The [GM](#gm) tells you **which stat** to use.
2. You roll **`1D20`**.
3. If the result is **lower than or equal to your stat value**, you succeed.
4. If the result is **higher**, or if you roll a **[critical failure](#critical-failure)** (`20`), the [action](#action) fails.

<u>Out-of-combat note</u>

Most [actions](#action) **do not require a [roll](#roll)** if you have a sufficient stat and no time pressure is involved.

### Penalties / Bonuses

**Bonuses** and **penalties** adjust the target value: a bonus **improves your chances of success**, while a penalty **reduces them**. Critical results are not affected.

<u>Examples of bonuses</u>

- an inattentive enemy;
- a relevant [talent](#talents);
- a bonus from equipment;
- an advantageous position, favorable terrain, or help from an ally.

<u>Examples of penalties</u>

- a serious injury;
- a very difficult task;
- an enemy that surprises you;
- muddy ground, darkness, panic, etc.

### Inspiration Points

**Inspiration Points** are awarded **only by the [GM](#gm)** to reward **creativity** and **[roleplay](#roleplay)**.

- If your action pleases the [GM](#gm) (`good idea`, `memorable scene`, `very funny moment`, `heroic risk-taking`, etc.), they may grant you **`1 inspiration point`**.
- **`1 inspiration point`** lets you **reroll one die** after a roll of your choice.

The [GM](#gm) is encouraged to distribute **`1 to 2` inspiration points** at the beginning of the session, after character creation, to players who showed:

- **imagination**;
- **humor**;
- **originality** in their concept or backstory.

## Calculation Rules

For all rolls and statistical calculations:

- **normal rounding** to the desired number of decimals;
- **in case of equality**: always round up.

> **Examples**:
>
> - `7.5 -> 8`
> - `12.49 -> 12`
> - `12.50 -> 13`

This rule applies to all systems in the game.

# Combat

## Action Points (AP)

**Action Points (AP)**: base of **`4 AP` per [turn](#turn)**. They determine the [actions](#action) you can perform each [turn](#turn). It is possible to save **`50 %` of unspent [AP](#action-points-ap)** for the next [turn](#turn). [AP](#action-points-ap) are recovered at the end of the [turn](#turn).

<u>Action Costs</u>

- **`1 AP`**: small action (`drink a potion`, `move a few steps`, `simple movement`, etc.).
- **`2 AP`**: single-target combat action (`attack`, `dodge`, `parry`, `run`, `shoot`, `throw`, etc.).
- **`3 AP`**: multi-target combat action or major single-target action.
- **`4 AP`**: major multi-target combat action.
- **`+1 AP`**: reaction (`requires a [roll](#roll) of [reflexes](#reflexes)` to act quickly against an enemy [action](#action)).
- **`0 AP`**: out-of-combat [actions](#action).

## Movement in Combat

Your movement is determined by your [speed](#speed) stat.

### Movement Distance (MD)

The **[movement distance](#movement-distance-md)** is the distance you can cover for **[`2 AP`](#action-points-ap)**.

`MD = Speed x 0.5 meter`

<u>Basic movement</u>

- Each [turn](#turn), you may move your full **[MD](#movement-distance-md)** for free.
- Beyond that distance, you may spend up to **[`2 AP`](#action-points-ap)** to regain **[MD](#movement-distance-md)**; each [AP](#action-points-ap) restores **half** of your **[MD](#movement-distance-md)**.

<u>Special movement</u>

- crossing an obstacle, jumping, or making an extra effort costs more **[MD](#movement-distance-md)**;
- moving through a difficult environment or terrain also costs more **[MD](#movement-distance-md)**.

<u>Opportunity attacks</u>

- If you do not pay **[`2 AP`](#action-points-ap)** to leave an enemy's striking zone, that enemy may make an **opportunity attack** against you.
- This rule also applies to enemies engaged with you in melee.

## Turn Order

Combat order is determined by [initiative](#initiative).

<u>Normal order</u>

- Characters act from highest [initiative](#initiative) score to lowest.
- **Tie between allies**: they may act simultaneously or determine order through discussion or a random draw.
- **Tie with an enemy**: order is fixed by a die roll at the start of combat and remains unchanged.

<u>Actions per [turn](#turn)</u>

- Each player may spend their **[AP](#action-points-ap)** and use their **[MD](#movement-distance-md)** freely.
- [AP](#action-points-ap) are recovered at the end of each [turn](#turn).
- It is possible to **delay your [turn](#turn)** to coordinate an attack with an ally or react to an enemy [action](#action).

<u>Surprise attack</u>

- attack roll bonus for the attackers;
- if the engagement is completely unexpected, the attackers play **their full turn before the defenders**;
- the order of [initiative](#initiative) is respected within each team.

## Attacks

In combat, an attack is never just a number: it depends on **what you do**, **how you do it**, and **what you exploit**.

<u>General principle</u>

An attack primarily depends on how many **[AP](#action-points-ap)** you choose to invest in it during that [turn](#turn).

- **`1 AP`**: light, fast, or opportunistic attack.
  > Example: quick strike, small cut, pommel hit, throwing a small object, distraction attack.
- **`2 AP`**: standard single-target attack.
  > Example: standard sword strike, precise shot, simple spell, short charge, well-executed attack.
- **`3 AP`**: heavy, technical attack or an attack targeting several nearby enemies.
  > Example: powerful blow, combo, sweeping strike, more complex offensive spell, attack combined with the environment.
- **`4 AP`**: major attack, major offensive action, or significant multi-target attack.
  > Example: full-force strike, area attack, large destructive move, long and risky maneuver.

<u>Resolving an attack</u>

1. The player describes their intention precisely and how many [AP](#action-points-ap) they invest in the attack, which determines its scale.
2. The [GM](#gm) determines the stat most suited to the [roll](#roll): often [strength](#strength), [dexterity](#dexterity), [intelligence](#intelligence), or [perception](#perception).
3. On a success, the [GM](#gm) evaluates the actual effect: damage, imbalance, loss of position, destruction of an object, area effect, etc.

<u>What increases an attack's effectiveness</u>

- exploiting an **obvious weakness** in the enemy;
- aiming at a vulnerable point or poorly protected area;
- taking advantage of terrain, a trap, height, fire, water, mud, a ledge, a chandelier, etc.;
- coordinating with an ally;
- surprising the opponent or forcing them to react from a bad position.

<u>What reduces an attack's effectiveness</u>

- a vague or unconvincing description;
- an unfavorable angle;
- a well-protected or hard-to-reach target;
- difficult terrain;
- an idea that ignores the physical, magical, or scene-based constraints.

<u>Weaknesses, affinities, and world logic</u>

- A well-chosen attack can be **more effective** without necessarily costing more [AP](#action-points-ap).
- A fire attack against a plant creature, a blunt attack against a fragile structure, or using water against a living flame can produce superior effects.
- Conversely, a bad approach can greatly reduce an attack's impact: stabbing a slime, burning a lava creature, or using a cutting strike against heavy armor is not always relevant.

<u>Environment and creativity</u>

- The [GM](#gm) is encouraged to **reward originality** in attacks.
- Using the environment intelligently should almost always be better than mechanically repeating the same attack every [turn](#turn).
- Dropping a beam, pushing an enemy into a brazier, toppling a shelf, throwing oil before lighting a fire, slamming a door on a target, or creating a diversion with an object can grant bonuses, increase damage, or create a special effect.

<u>Intention rule</u>

The more an attack shows that the player listened to the [GM](#gm)'s description, understood the scene, and thought about the situation, the more likely it is to be interesting, effective, or memorable.

<u>The GM's role</u>

- The [GM](#gm) decides whether the attack deals only damage or also causes a narrative or tactical effect.
- They may grant a bonus, improve the effect, or award an [inspiration point](#inspiration-points) for a particularly inventive attack.
- They may also apply a penalty, an extra cost, or a risk if the action is too ambitious for the situation.

## Weapons

<u>Weapon types</u>

- **Two-handed weapons** (or double weapons): deal high damage (`greatsword`, `halberd`, `bow`, etc.).
- **One-handed weapons**: deal medium damage (`dagger`, `short sword`, `saber`, etc.).
- **Shield**: adds [AR](#armor-ar), provided the [strength](#strength) requirement is met, and makes blocking enemy attacks easier.

<u>AR granted by a shield</u>

- **Common**: `5% max HP`
- **Uncommon**: `10% max HP`
- **Rare**: `15% max HP`
- **Epic**: `20% max HP`
- **Legendary**: `25% max HP`

The [GM](#gm) may adjust this value slightly depending on the shield's size, shape, or special effect, but these numbers serve as a simple and quick baseline.

<u>Weapons for mages</u>

- Mages do not need a focus to use their [RULE](#rule).
- Appropriate equipment can, however, strengthen a [RULE](#rule)'s abilities (`a torch` for a fire RULE, `a shuriken` for a wind RULE, etc.).
- It is possible to carry a normal weapon for last-resort close combat defense.
- Some artifacts (`books`, etc.) may be used to reinforce a particular magical practice, subject to [GM](#gm) approval.

<u>Status effects</u>

- Some weapons and abilities can inflict special effects (`poison`, `stun`, `slow`, etc.).

## Defense, Saving, and Survival

In combat, you have several options when you are attacked:

- **Dodge** ([dexterity](#dexterity)) or **block** ([strength](#strength)): you may try up to **`2` times per [turn](#turn)** to avoid an attack. Each attempt costs **[`2 AP`](#action-points-ap)**, deducted from your next [turn](#turn).
- **Defensive magic** ([intelligence](#intelligence)): if your **[RULE](#rule)** allows it, you may use magic to protect yourself. The possibilities depend entirely on your RULE's description:
  - proactively, by creating a **magic shield** during your [turn](#turn);
  - reactively during the enemy [turn](#turn), like a dodge or block, for example by deflecting a projectile with a gust of wind;
  - afterward, by **healing** an injured ally.
- **Take the hit**: suffer the damage directly, which is absorbed first by your armor and then by your [HP](#hit-points-hp).

### Hit Points (HP)

Your **hit points** represent your survival reserve and are calculated as:

`HP = Constitution × 10`

When your [HP](#hit-points-hp) drop to `0`, you are **out of combat** and only have **`1 AP` per [turn](#turn)** left. Each time you take new damage in that state, you must succeed on a **[roll](#roll) of [constitution](#constitution)** to stay alive. On a failure, your character dies. This state ends if you are healed or when the fight ends.

## Armor and Protection

### Armor (AR)

**[Armor](#armor-ar)** functions like bonus [HP](#hit-points-hp). Once it drops to `0`, damage is applied directly to your [HP](#hit-points-hp). **[AR](#armor-ar)** is determined by your maximum [HP](#hit-points-hp) and your equipment.

Each armor is designed to absorb physical and magical damage. The level of **protection** varies based on rarity, craftsmanship, and condition.

<u>Armor types by [constitution](#constitution)</u>

- **Light** (`Constitution >= 0`): `0 to 45 %` of [HP](#hit-points-hp) converted into armor.
  - common: `5 %`
  - uncommon: `15 %`
  - rare: `25 %`
  - epic: `35 %`
  - legendary: `45 %`
- **Medium** (`Constitution >= 12`): `20 to 65 %` of [HP](#hit-points-hp) converted into armor.
  - common: `25 %`
  - uncommon: `35 %`
  - rare: `45 %`
  - epic: `55 %`
  - legendary: `65 %`
- **Heavy** (`Constitution >= 14`): `40 to 85 %` of [HP](#hit-points-hp) converted into armor.
  - common: `45 %`
  - uncommon: `55 %`
  - rare: `65 %`
  - epic: `75 %`
  - legendary: `85 %`

<u>Exceptions</u>

Armor offers no protection against:

- critical hits;
- piercing damage aimed at unprotected areas;
- persistent effects (`poison`, `disease`, etc.).

## Enchantments and Runes

A **rune** is a magical inscription that transfers part of the power of a [RULE](#rule) into an object, a structure, or a device.

A rune does not create power and never transfers a full [RULE](#rule). It fixes one precise function of that [RULE](#rule).

Each object can receive **`one rune per RULE`**.

### 1. A RULE to Transfer

To create a rune, you must possess the [RULE](#rule) corresponding to the desired effect.

The level of mastery of the [RULE](#rule) determines the enchantment's **maximum power**. The creator chooses which part of their power they want to transfer and loses no power by making the rune.

> **Examples**: a fire RULE may produce a flaming aura around a sword or a trap that launches a fireball.

### 2. Magical Materials

Creating a rune requires a **magical material**. This material is consumed during creation, then remains integrated into the enchanted object or device.

Each material has a **transmission rate**, which determines how much power the rune can preserve. The more material is used, the more powerful the rune becomes. Using more material also makes the rune larger.

Materials are classified by rarity:

- **Common**: `5 %` transmission
- **Uncommon**: `10 %` transmission
- **Rare**: `20 %` transmission
- **Epic**: `40 %` transmission
- **Legendary**: `80 %` transmission

The [GM](#gm) and the players determine together which materials exist, their rarity, and their transmission rates. It is discouraged to use **simple rare metals**, or at least to leave them at `common` / `uncommon`, in order to avoid creating **thousands of enchanted weapons** too easily and **on a large scale**.

> **Examples**: gold and diamond may be `common` or `uncommon`, while the rest may be made of materials taken from rare magical creatures.

### 3. Runic Art and Language

**Runic art** allows a rune to be inscribed. Knowing a [RULE](#rule) is not enough: one must also master the runic language.

A rune may be created by several people: one person provides the [RULE](#rule), while another masters the carving and stabilization.

> **Examples**: a runesmith may forge a rune onto a blade, paint one onto a door, embroider one onto fabric, etc.

### Creating a Rune

<u>1. Choose the function</u>

The creator precisely defines the rune's function, as well as its limits, range, duration, and possible trigger.

They must also choose whether the rune will be **always active** or **activatable**.

> **Examples**: strengthening armor against fire or producing light when the item is activated.

<u>2. Choose the support</u>

The rune may be carved onto equipment, a weapon, armor, jewelry, a door, a trap, a wall, or any other suitable support.

The support must be able to withstand the transmitted power.

<u>3. Prepare the magical material</u>

The magical material is prepared and then integrated into the support. It becomes a permanent part of the enchantment.

<u>4. Inscribe the rune</u>

The creator inscribes the rune according to the rules of the runic language. A mistake can make the enchantment unstable or unusable.

<u>5. Transfer the power</u>

The creator uses their [RULE](#rule) to transfer part of their power into the material and the rune.

The rune remains active even after its creator's death.

<u>Enchantments rarity</u>

The rarity of an enchantment depends on:

- the level of the [RULE](#rule) used;
- the rarity and quantity of the material;
- the quality of the inscription.

It influences:

- the **power** of the effect;
- its range or area;
- its duration;
- the number of targets affected;
- the rune's resistance.

> **Simple guideline**:
>
> - **Common**: small simple effect, short range, or low intensity, often utility-focused.
> - **Uncommon**: stable, useful effect that is already notable on an item or device.
> - **Rare**: powerful or versatile effect, able to clearly influence a fight or a scene.
> - **Epic**: very powerful, long-lasting, or difficult-to-reproduce effect, often with a strong magical identity.
> - **Legendary**: exceptional effect, rare on a world scale, potentially central to a quest, place, or relic.

Enchantments can be **common**, **uncommon**, **rare**, **epic**, or **legendary**. The [GM](#gm) determines their final rarity.

### Using a Rune

There are two types of runes.

<u>Always-active runes</u>

An always-active rune produces its effect permanently, or as long as its support remains in good condition.

It costs no [AP](#action-points-ap) and has no cooldown. In return, its effect is fixed and cannot be controlled or modified by the user.

> **Examples**: a fire aura around a sword or a barrier that constantly protects a place.

<u>Activatable runes</u>

An activatable rune produces no effect until triggered. Once activated, its user can usually control its effect.

Its activation may require:

- [AP](#action-points-ap);
- a cooldown;
- a specific condition;
- or several of these elements.

The more powerful, complex, or frequent the effect is, the greater its cost or cooldown.

> **Examples**: a staff that fires lightning at a target or a tile that triggers a fireball when a creature steps on it.

An activatable rune may be used as many times as desired as long as its support remains in good condition.

## Fixed Runes and Devices

Fixed runes are runes integrated into a place or device: trap, door, lock, alarm, seal, or barrier.

They are generally activatable and have a trigger defined during creation:

- contact;
- pressure;
- command word;
- opening a door;
- passage of a creature;
- intrusion into an area.

A fixed rune can be powerful thanks to a large amount of material, but its operation remains limited by its position, range, and trigger.

> **Examples**: a tile may trigger an explosion when pressed, or a door may open after hearing a precise word.

## Identifying a Rune

A character may attempt to identify a rune by studying its carving, support, material, or the energy it gives off.

A success may reveal the rune's function or the material used. It may also allow discovery of its rarity, its source [RULE](#rule), its trigger, or its limits.

# Adventure

## Resting, Camping, and Healing

<u>Passive recovery</u>

- Requires being **out of combat**.
- Regeneration of **[AR](#armor-ar)** and **[HP](#hit-points-hp)** is limited to **`50 %`** of maximum [HP](#hit-points-hp)/[AR](#armor-ar).

<u>Full recovery</u>

- Requires setting up camp and spending the night.
- Fully regenerates **[AR](#armor-ar)**.
- Restores all **[HP](#hit-points-hp)** (`except serious injuries`).
- Heals fatigue and minor injuries.
- In hostile areas (`dungeons`, etc.):
  - ambush risks;
  - if no watch is kept: risk of surprise attack;
  - if watches are kept: regeneration of [AR](#armor-ar) and [HP](#hit-points-hp) is limited to **`80 %`**.

<u>Serious injuries or healing in combat</u>

- Require the use of **healing potions** or **healing magic**.
- Alternative: go to an **allied town** to obtain them.

## Social Interactions

Confrontation is not always the best solution. Use your [charisma](#charisma) to:

- **persuade** guards or obtain information;
- **intimidate** an enemy to avoid a fight;
- **negotiate** with merchants to get better prices.

[NPCs](#npc) encountered during the adventure may hold **crucial information** for your quest. Take the time to talk with them.

<u>Buying system</u>

- **merchants** offer potions, equipment, and miscellaneous items;
- **negotiation** is recommended: discounts are possible depending on your [charisma](#charisma);
- **preparation** is essential: stock up on resources before setting out on an adventure;
- there are different types of merchants: generalists, specialists ([weapons](#weapons), [armor](#armor-and-protection), potions), or illegal dealers.

## Loot and Rewards

<u>On defeated enemies</u>

- **Realistic system**: you recover only what the enemy was carrying.
- **Variable equipment** depending on enemy type and rank.
- **Personal items**: letters, keys, and quest objects may sometimes be found.

<u>Chests and treasures</u>

- **Random generation**: every opening is a surprise.
- **Progressive rarity**: tombs and deep dungeons contain more valuable objects.
- **Associated risks**: traps, curses, or guardian creatures are possible.
- **Useful skills**: detection to spot traps, lockpicking to open locks.

<u>Loot rarity</u>

This section applies global rarity to discovered rewards. Here, rarity mainly gives an approximation of the **level**, **value**, and **quality** of what is found, not necessarily its exact price.

- **Common**: standard equipment, basic potions, small amounts of money.
- **Uncommon**: solid equipment, useful resources, specialized items, or loot slightly above normal.
- **Rare**: superior [weapons](#weapons)/[armor](#armor-and-protection), strong potions, minor artifacts.
- **Epic**: remarkable objects, very rare equipment, powerful relics.
- **Legendary**: unique objects, ancient artifacts, exceptional equipment.

## Economy

The economy should remain **simple**, **consistent**, and **quick to arbitrate**. The goal is not to simulate a market in detail, but to give the [GM](#gm) reliable guidelines for setting item values.

<u>General rule</u>

The [GM](#gm) always sets the final value based on rarity, location, supply, demand, urgency, and the seller's or buyer's reputation.

<u>Rarity levels and general value</u>

- **Very common**: ordinary everyday object, easy to find almost anywhere.
- **Common**: useful, classic object available in most villages or towns.
- **Uncommon**: good-quality object, specialized or somewhat harder to obtain.
- **Rare**: sought-after object, difficult to find, or of high quality.
- **Epic**: exceptional, very rare object, often remarkable or magical.
- **Legendary**: unique or near-unique object, beyond the norm, rarely available for purchase.

<u>Price guidelines</u>

- **Very common**: `1 to 5` gold pieces for a simple everyday object.
- **Common**: `5 to 15` gold pieces for a useful tool, a simple weapon, or common gear.
- **Uncommon**: `15 to 40` gold pieces for good equipment, a specialized weapon, or a basic armor piece.
- **Rare**: `40 to 150` gold pieces for an unusual, high-quality, or hard-to-get item.
- **Epic**: `150 to 500` gold pieces for a remarkable, powerful, or hard-to-replace object.
- **Legendary**: `500+` gold pieces, or a price freely set by the [GM](#gm), often only through a quest, debt, favor, or exceptional situation.

<u>Useful benchmarks</u>

- a standard **healing potion** usually costs between `10` and `25` gold pieces depending on region and availability;
- a **basic weapon** usually costs between `8` and `20` gold pieces;
- a **basic armor** usually costs between `15` and `40` gold pieces depending on type;
- purely cosmetic items, simple clothes, or small accessories cost little, unless they are luxurious or rare.

## Trade

Trade should remain **quick to play** and **easy to arbitrate**. This section explains how to buy, sell, negotiate, and find the right buyer.

<u>Negotiation</u>

- A character may attempt **a [roll](#roll) of [charisma](#charisma)** during a major purchase, a major sale, or a particularly tense trade discussion.
- On a success, the [GM](#gm) may grant a discount or a better sale price of about **`5 % to 20 %`**.
- On a failure, the price usually remains unchanged.
- On a **[critical success](#critical-success)**, the merchant may make an exceptional gesture, give useful information, or add a small trade bonus.
- On a **[critical failure](#critical-failure)**, the merchant may take offense, refuse the transaction, raise the price, or spread a bad reputation.

<u>Buying</u>

- The player describes what they are looking for, then the [GM](#gm) decides whether the item is available depending on location, rarity, and context.
- If the item is common, the transaction can be almost immediate.
- If the item is rare, illegal, custom-made, or magical, the [GM](#gm) may require time, a specific contact, an order, a favor, or a narrative step.
- For minor purchases, there is no need to make a [roll](#roll), unless the scene has a real stake.

<u>Selling an item</u>

- As a general rule, a merchant buys an item for less than its theoretical value.
- A very sought-after, rare, or perfectly preserved item may be bought for more.
- A damaged, dirty, compromised, or hard-to-resell item may be bought for much less, or even refused.

<u>Finding the right buyer</u>

- A general merchant easily buys common items, but rarely pays very well.
- A specialist may better value a weapon, [armor](#armor-and-protection), a rare book, or an artifact.
- A collector, noble, or mage may pay far more for a very specific object, but you must reach them first.

<u>Availability by location</u>

- a **small village** mainly sells common equipment;
- a **large city** offers more choice, more volume, and more stable prices;
- a **specialist merchant** sells better, more expensive goods, but with stronger guarantees;
- a **black market** can provide rare, forbidden, or suspicious items, often at a high price and with narrative risks.

## Inventory Weight Management

Encumbrance should remain **simple**. Exact weight is not counted for every item: the focus is on what actually hinders movement, carrying, and action.

### Principle

Each character has an **encumbrance capacity** equal to their **[constitution](#constitution)**.

> **Example**: a character with `10` in [constitution](#constitution) can carry up to **`10 encumbrance points`** without penalty.

### Encumbrance Value of Items

- **`0 points`**: tiny negligible items taken individually (`ring`, `coin purse`, `lighter`, `empty vial`, `letter`, `key`, etc.), light armor, clothes, ammunition, and gold (`in reasonable quantities`).
- **`1 point`**: light weapon, medium armor, tool, small useful reserve, lantern, rope, book, potion, ration, dagger, small bag.
- **`2 points`**: main weapon, shield, heavy armor, large package, bulky kit, large supply of gear, bow, spear, heavy axe.
- **`3 points`**: very large object, light chest, awkward load, body being dragged, full large sack.

The [GM](#gm) decides in case of doubt. If an item is bulky, awkward, or hard to handle, it should cost more encumbrance than an item that is merely heavy.

### Encumbrance Thresholds

- **Up to normal capacity**: no penalty.
- **Capacity + `1` to `2`**: character is **loaded**.
  - `-1` to actions tied to [speed](#speed), [initiative](#initiative), and [reflexes](#reflexes);
  - **[MD](#movement-distance-md)** reduced by **`25 %`**;
  - **[AP](#action-points-ap)** reduced by **`1`**.
- **Capacity + `3` to `4`**: character is **heavily loaded**.
  - `-2` to actions tied to [speed](#speed), [initiative](#initiative), and [reflexes](#reflexes);
  - **[MD](#movement-distance-md)** reduced by **`50 %`**;
  - **[AP](#action-points-ap)** reduced by **`2`**.
- **Beyond capacity + `4`**: **excessive load**.
  - the character can no longer fight properly, run, or travel for long without help;
  - the [GM](#gm) may require them to drop gear, use a mount, a cart, or help from another character.

### Practical Rules

- A character may hand off, store, tie down, or redistribute their gear to avoid penalties.
- Outside [combat](#combat), the [GM](#gm) may soften encumbrance if the scene has no immediate stakes.

### Spirit of the Rule

Encumbrance management mainly exists to prevent obvious abuses: carrying **everything**, **all the time**, with no consequences. As long as the players remain reasonable, there is no need to count every detail precisely.

## Character Progression

Your character starts at **level `1`**. The maximum level is **`10`**. The [GM](#gm) decides when players level up and judges when the party has earned enough experience to deserve it.

<u>Level `1`</u>

- **`67 points`** of stats to distribute.
- **`1 talent`**.

<u>Rewards per level</u>

- **At every level**:
  - **`2 points`** to distribute between stats and RULE;
  - **`+20 %`** of maximum [HP](#hit-points-hp).
- **Levels `2`, `5`, and `8`**: `+1` bonus stat point.
- **Levels `3`, `6`, and `9`**: `+1` new talent.
- **Levels `4` and `7`**: `+1 AP`.
- **Level `10`**: `+2 AP`.

## Death and Character Replacement

<u>Consequences of death</u>

- **Creating a new character** is mandatory.
- **Lower level**: the new character is **one level below** the other players.
- **Quick catch-up**: the gap is closed after about **`1 to 2 sessions`** of play.

<u>Irreversibility of death</u>

- **No resurrection** is possible in the standard system.
- **Extremely rare exceptions**: legendary artifacts only.
- **Near-permanent death**: each death has lasting consequences.

<u>Creating the new character</u>

- Same creation rules as an initial character.
- Narrative integration should be discussed with the [GM](#gm).

# Advice for the Game Master

## Role and Responsibilities of the Game Master

### Primary Mission: Balance

The [GM](#gm) is the guarantor of **balance** and **consistency** in the game. They must:

- remain **objective** in all circumstances;
- grant **maximum freedom** to players in their choices;
- maintain **impartiality** in their decisions.

### Character Creation Supervision

The [GM](#gm) must **actively supervise** every character creation:

- check the **consistency** of talents, RULEs, races, and backstories;
- ensure **balance** between characters;
- **correct** unbalanced or incoherent choices when necessary;
- validate the **fit** between the character concept and the rules.

### Running the Game

The [GM](#gm) must remain **impartial** in all decisions concerning:

- the behavior of **enemies** and **NPCs**;
- the distribution of **loot** and rewards;
- the interpretation of **rules** and ambiguous situations.

### Adjustments and Self-Review

- **Constant rebalancing**: the [GM](#gm) must correct their balance mistakes without hesitation.
- **Owning mistakes**: they must admit their errors and listen to **player feedback**.
- **Flexibility**: they adapt the game according to feedback and play experience.

### Relationship with Players

- **Constant dialogue**: maintain open communication with the group.
- **Fair arbitration**: resolve conflicts impartially.
- **Respect for fun**: prioritize an enjoyable play experience for everyone.

# Definitions

<a id="ttrpg"></a>
<u>TTRPG</u>

**Tabletop roleplaying game**: an activity in which a person plays the role of a character, real or imaginary, within a fictional or simulated world.

<a id="gm"></a>
<u>GM</u>

**Game Master**: the person who prepares the session, describes the world, plays non-player characters, arbitrates the rules, and decides the consequences of the players' actions.

<a id="pc"></a>
<u>PC</u>

**Player character**: a character portrayed by a player during the game.

<a id="npc"></a>
<u>NPC</u>

**Non-player character**: a character controlled by the [GM](#gm), such as a merchant, a guard, a king, an intelligent monster, or a temporary ally.

<a id="campaign"></a>
<u>Campaign</u>

A series of sessions connected by a shared story, characters, and setting.

<a id="session"></a>
<u>Session</u>

A single game meeting, corresponding to a specific time when the group gathers to play.

<a id="one-shot"></a>
<u>One-shot</u>

A short game, generally played in a single session, with a complete story.

<a id="lore"></a>
<u>Lore</u>

The set of information about the game world: history, peoples, places, religions, conflicts, legends, etc.

<a id="background"></a>
<u>Background</u>

A character's past, origin, and personal history before the adventure begins.

<a id="roll"></a>
<u>Roll</u>

The act of rolling a die to determine whether an attempt succeeds, fails, or produces a particular result.

<a id="critical-success"></a>
<u>Critical success</u>

An exceptionally favorable result. In this system, **`1`** generally corresponds to a critical success.

<a id="critical-failure"></a>
<u>Critical failure</u>

An exceptionally unfavorable result. In this system, **`20`** generally corresponds to a critical failure.

<a id="turn"></a>
<u>Turn</u>

The moment during combat when a character can act, spend actions, and make choices.

<a id="round"></a>
<u>Round</u>

A full combat cycle during which all participants have had the opportunity to take a turn.

<a id="action"></a>
<u>Action</u>

What a character does during their turn: attack, run, cast a spell, drink a potion, etc.

<a id="alignment"></a>
<u>Alignment</u>

A moral or behavioral reference point used to describe how a character generally acts.

<a id="pvp"></a>
<u>PvP</u>

**Player versus player**: a situation in which two player characters directly oppose each other.

<a id="metagame"></a>
<u>Metagame</u>

Using, as a player, information that your character should not know within the game world.
